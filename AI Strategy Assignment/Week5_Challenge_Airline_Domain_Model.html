"Just Fine-Tune It on Our Manuals"

How do you actually build a domain-specific language model? A real problem from the airline industry, in four tasks.

Teams of 3–4 35–40 minutes Deliverable: one-page recommendation
The scenario

The VP's request

A major U.S. airline wants a language model that "knows the airline". General-purpose LLMs don't know its fleet, its procedures, or how its mechanics write logbook entries. The VP of Engineering has a plan:

"Take an 8-billion-parameter open model, fine-tune it on our 10 GB of maintenance manuals, logbooks, and operating procedures, and it will know our airline."— VP of Engineering

You are the airline's Gen AI team. The VP wants your recommendation by the end of the meeting. Is the plan right, partly right, or wrong? What would you build instead?

Before you start: read the Week 5 lecture note (training stages, the Chinchilla rule, the 6ND compute rule). Everything you need for this challenge is there.

What's in the 10 GB

Maintenance manualsStep-by-step procedures, organized by ATA chapter (e.g., 32 = landing gear, 49 = APU). Many are PDFs, some scanned.
Aircraft logbooksMillions of short, abbreviation-heavy defect and repair entries, with dates, tail numbers, and part numbers.
Operating proceduresDispatch, flight operations, and the MEL (the list of what may be inoperative on a flight). Revised on a regular schedule.
Safety & irregular-ops reportsFree-text narratives written by crews, dispatchers, and station staff.
Your tasks

Four tasks

1

Match each requirement to a technique

~12 min

The airline needs the model to do the six things below. For each one, pick the technique (or combination) that fits best, and justify it in one sentence. More than one technique can be right. Explain your choice.

Continued pretraining Instruction fine-tuning Classification fine-tuning Preference alignment (DPO) RAG NL-to-SQL Prompting only
#RequirementTechniqueWhy (one sentence)
aUnderstand logbook shorthand such as L/H MLG TIRE WORN TO LIMITS, REPLACED IAW AMM 32-xx
bAnswer "What is the current MEL relief for an inoperative APU on our 737 fleet?" and cite the source document
cRoute each new defect write-up to the correct ATA chapter
dSummarize a shift's irregular-operations reports in the dispatch team's standard format
e"How many tire replacements did the 787 fleet have last quarter?"
fNever imply it can sign off on maintenance; say "I don't know" when it isn't sure
Think about: Is the requirement about language (vocabulary, style), facts (which may change), a task (a fixed input→output behavior), behavior (what it refuses), or data that lives in tables?
2

Do the math

~8 min
  1. Roughly how many tokens is 10 GB of clean English text? Why might 10 GB of files contain much less text than that?
  2. Could you pretrain a 3B-parameter model from scratch on this data? Use the Chinchilla rule of thumb.
  3. Roughly how many GPU-hours would one epoch of continued pretraining of an 8B model on this data take?
  4. Based on your answers, where is the real cost of this project: compute, or something else?
# Useful numbers English text (BPE) ≈ 4 bytes per token Chinchilla rule of thumb ≈ 20 training tokens per parameter Training compute ≈ 6 × N (parameters) × D (tokens) FLOPs One H100, realistic speed ≈ 4 × 10¹⁴ FLOP/s (about 40% of peak)
3

Design the evaluation first

~8 min

Before anyone trains anything, how will you know whether a trained model is better than what the airline could get without training?

  • What goes into your test set, and who writes it?
  • Which baselines would you compare against? Name at least three.
  • What result would make you stop and not train a model at all?
4

Name the biggest risk

~5 min

This is a safety-regulated industry, and procedures are revised regularly. What is the biggest risk of putting procedures into the model's weights, and how would you design around it? Name one more risk that has nothing to do with the model (think about the data itself).

Deliverable (one page per team): your Task 1 table, your numbers from Task 2, your evaluation plan, your top risk, and a one-sentence recommendation to the VP. Be ready to defend it in 2 minutes.
Reference

The techniques, in one line each

Continued pretraining: keep training a pretrained model on raw domain text with the same next-token objective. No labels needed.
Instruction fine-tuning: train on (instruction, response) pairs so the model performs tasks in a desired format.
Classification fine-tuning: replace the output layer with a small head and train it to predict a fixed set of labels.
Preference alignment (DPO): train on pairs of preferred vs. rejected answers to shape behavior.
RAG: retrieve relevant documents at question time and put them in the prompt. The model's weights don't change.
NL-to-SQL: translate a natural-language question into a database query and run it.
